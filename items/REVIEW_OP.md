## Verified by search
1. Air medical landing zone (Air Methods LZ guidance). Firm, flat, obstacle-free site; never shine light at the pilot; approach only on a crew signal and within the pilot's view; never go behind the aircraft (tail rotor); keep bystanders well back. This supports OP-031 as keyed.
2. Bloodborne pathogen exposure (CDC REACH job aid, which follows the OSHA standard). Irrigate the eyes with water or saline, report promptly to the supervisor, get an immediate confidential medical evaluation, then lab testing, prophylaxis when indicated, and counseling. This supports the OP-021 order.
3. TB transport precautions (NYS DOH EMS policy 13-05; JEMS review of CDC guidance). For suspected or confirmed TB, the crew wears N95 respirators, the patient wears a surgical mask, and outside-air circulation is maximized. This supports OP-022. JEMS notes that CDC does not require N95s for routine transport of some respiratory illnesses. That does not apply here, because the patient has classic active TB signs and the airborne-precaution key is standard.
4. Elder abuse mandatory reporting (Stetson and FSU state statute compilations). Reporting duties exist across states but differ in detail. This confirmed that the OP-008 key should defer to state law, which it already does.
